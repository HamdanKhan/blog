When Algorithms Take You Back: A Late-Night Walk Through My Musical Memory

Sunday, December 21, 2025

Last night, my sleep lost its way. Rather than forcing it, I did what many of us do—I opened YouTube and searched for a few instrumentals, hoping the music would quiet my mind.

What happened next was familiar, yet still fascinating.

YouTube’s algorithm began doing what it does best: curating. Slowly, my feed filled with music I had listened to years ago. One video led to another, and before I realized it, I had gone several layers deep. It struck me how effective these algorithms have become—not just at keeping us engaged, but at hooking us emotionally. At the same time, the experience felt almost symbiotic. I enjoyed being taken down memory lane, rediscovering tracks I once played on repeat, each carrying its own quiet associations.

My relationship with music goes back a long way.

Growing up, my mamo—my paternal uncle—ran what I can best describe as a custom cassette recording shop. I am not entirely sure how legal that operation was, but as a child, the perks were undeniable. We always had access to a solid collection of good music. Later, when I was old enough to visit the market myself, I would buy pre-recorded collections. Then came computers, and everything shifted to MP3s, CDs, and eventually massive digital libraries downloaded from online sources.

Technology changed the medium, but not the habit.

Today, I do pay for a Spotify account—although it is technically for my brother. That said, music is not something I depend on every night to fall asleep. Many times, especially at work, I listen to the Quran, naats, or bayans instead. For me, this has never felt contradictory. If you are surprised by the coexistence of music and Islamic content in the same routine, it really should not be. I have moved between the two comfortably for years.

I change sides regularly, without guilt or confusion.

Perhaps that is what made last night’s experience meaningful. It was not just about an algorithm doing its job well; it was about how fragments of our past—songs, sounds, and memories—remain quietly stored, waiting to be resurfaced by the right trigger. Sometimes, all it takes is a sleepless night and a search bar.

And sometimes, the machine understands you better than you expect.
