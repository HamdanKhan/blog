Learning

Sunday, January 18, 2026

I started this post with the free version of Grammarly. I don’t know if I should commit more money to this blog with a Grammarly subscription, but at least it’s allowing me to keep my own words. To be fair, Claude also did that to an extent, i.e., keep the write-up as original, but if you instruct other AI services, they will do the same. In this blog, all posts from 2025 onwards were written using Gemini Pro, Claude, and ChatGPT. Not in the complete sense, of course—they cannot contribute stories that are personal, but they do speed up the process, so now I need to decide between losing the original words and letting AI rewrite everything, or do Iktifa (contentment) with what my brain can produce with Grammarly. As I am the only reader of this blog, this doesn’t change much; better still, I can see more ways to write the same idea.

Now, back to the concern of wasting money, I bought a Coursera subscription with the intent to complete the Mathematics for Data Science course. Normally, if I had not paid for the subscription and had only audited it, it would have taken years to complete the course. Now I put at least a few minutes a day into it. As I share the commitment to the course here, it will serve as a good blog post when I complete it.

The last thing I will write here is about Shab-e-Miraj. I know it was a “big night,” also witnessed from people going the extra mile with prayers, but I was looking for a complete post on the topic. Now I’m only deciding to write about the different beliefs people here have about that night. No one cannot deny it, as it is mentioned in the Quran, but some say that it was a dream (Ghamdi). Some don’t agree on the dates when it happened and how late it happened in the Islamic calendar, as the prayers were made mandatory; some people say it was early when the preaching started 5th year, according to Yasir Qadhi.
